NASA Earth observation datasets

Key terms

Satellite vs. ground measurements

Satellite records (like SMAP soil moisture or ECOSTRESS land‑surface temperature) give consistent, wide‑area coverage but at coarser resolution and with indirect retrievals. Ground sensors and farmer observation are precise locally but sparse in space and time. Combining both — resampling satellite layers to a common grid and anchoring them with local readings — gives more trustworthy signals than either alone.

How the comparison works

Each candidate crop carries illustrative scores (0–5) across water demand, nitrogen fixing, erosion reduction, organic matter contribution, market value, frost tolerance, and pest‑cycle disruption. A rotation's score is the weighted average of its crops' scores against your saved priority weights. This is a transparent heuristic, not a calibrated agronomic model — it is meant to surface options and trade‑offs worth discussing with a local agronomist, not to replace that judgment.

This tool supports — it does not replace — local expertise

Every rotation card lists risks and information gaps that need review by the farmer and local agricultural experts before acting on them.
